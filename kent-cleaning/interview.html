STEP 01

1. Tell the story of the business

A 30–45 minute interview. Answer what you know; leave unknowns visible. Use ranges for money and document descriptions rather than uploading tax returns, account numbers, or identity documents.

STEP 02

2. General preparation score

An original planning rubric—not an SBA score, credit score, valuation or approval prediction. Ten equally weighted areas: unknown = 0, needs work = 0, draft = 5, evidence ready = 10. The result measures documented preparation only; no pass/fail threshold.

STEP 03

3. SBA loan conversation

SBA-backed financing is a lender decision. A lender checks program eligibility, credit and repayment capacity; this page cannot access credit reports or calculate an official lending score. A complete checklist does not mean eligibility or approval. Start by asking whether a loan is actually needed.

SBA 7(a): eligibility and how to apply · Find SBA resource-partner counseling. The SBA describes free or low-cost local counseling; confirm any service fee before booking.

STEP 04

4. A portfolio from real experience

Build a private work history first. Then choose a few strong examples for a public portfolio. Record all known work over time; this page does not claim the history is complete. Use client aliases until you have permission to identify them. Past prospects are not past customers.

STEP 05

5. Form the business around the work

Discuss these options with a CPA and a Minnesota business attorney. Choosing a state legal entity and choosing federal tax treatment are different decisions. No structure automatically produces the lowest total tax or cost.

Current setup

Document what exists before changing it: owners, registrations, contracts, insurance, taxes and bookkeeping.

LLC option

Minnesota permits formation through Articles of Organization. Federal tax treatment depends on owners and elections. Compare liability needs, administration and annual total costs.

Corporation / S election

A corporation is a legal form; S treatment is a tax election available to eligible businesses. S corporations must pay reasonable compensation for shareholder-employee services before non-wage distributions. Model payroll and preparation costs before deciding.

Minnesota lists carpet cleaning as a taxable service. Ask a tax professional to review registration, invoicing, applicable rates and any past corrections. The IRS issues EINs free directly; compare official filing costs before purchasing a formation package.
