Available now

Something you can explore.

  • A public website that opens without ChatGPT, an installation, or an account.
  • Nine example profiles, from a $5 meal to a $10,000 small business idea, plus gifts of time, items, and prayer.
  • Fourteen resource links, with practical help beside each story.
  • Personal need drafts and giving plans saved only on your own device.
  • A preview of direct and organization-coordinated help, with named or anonymous giving preferences.
The next step

A small pilot, with real care.

Invite a willing parish, charity, or fiscal sponsor to shape a pilot together. Agree on who reviews requests, supports recipients, handles money, and reports how funds are used.

Then build the shared service around those decisions, beginning with one community and a manageable number of participants.

Current stage: a working public preview. No organization has agreed to operate it yet, and no donations are being accepted here.

What support would help build next

A practical sequence for discussing funding and responsibilities with a participating organization.

Agree on the pilot

Choose a community, name the responsible organization, define its role, and prepare a scoped budget with that organization.

Connect real participants

Add independent sign-in, a shared database, private request review, reporting, and a coordinator’s workspace.

Make giving possible

Set up approved payment and delivery arrangements, named or anonymous gifts, clear fees, and accurate records through the agreed operator.

Learn and improve

Run a small pilot, listen to recipients and donors, keep resource links current, and publish useful measures of help delivered.

Ways to contribute

Introduce a possible operating organization. Review the idea with someone who has asked for help. Offer design, accessibility, software, or community coordination experience.

People interested in funding can use this page to discuss the next milestones. A funding target can be set after the pilot scope, operator, and costs are agreed.

A network worth trusting

People choose what to share. Requests get respectful review. Recipients are never required to perform gratitude or religious participation.

The future anonymous option would hide the donor from the recipient, while the responsible organization and payment provider retain the records they need.

Bring the idea into a conversation.

Share this page with someone you care about. Ask: Would this be useful? What would help people feel respected? Do we know an organization that might want to shape a small pilot with us?
