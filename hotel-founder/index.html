01 / Working founder profile

A direction to test and refine

This draft reflects the first conversation. It is a planning hypothesis that the founder can correct as she learns.

Starting vision

Explore a smaller bed and breakfast as the first ownership goal, with a focus on what its daily operations and guest experience would require.

Long view

Keep an adults only resort or larger hotel in a busier tourist area as a later possibility. There is no need to choose that second destination this month.

Month One victory

Know where to start: choose a practical first learning route, write down unknowns, and make a short next steps plan.

Planning rhythm

Make progress visible through one small action and one review each week. Record what was learned, what remains uncertain, and what to do next.

02 / Month One preparation map

Four weeks, four useful decisions

Move from an idea to a reasoned starting point. These are research and planning exercises, not opening tasks.

WEEK 1

Define the first version

Describe the B&B you might want: approximate room count, guest type, location possibilities, and what you want to do yourself.

Output: a one paragraph concept and three questions.
WEEK 2

Learn the ownership routes

Compare buying an existing B&B, converting a property, and starting as an operator or manager first. Note the skills each path requires.

Output: a simple option comparison. Open Week 2 →
WEEK 3

Sketch the numbers

List likely startup costs, ongoing expenses, room revenue assumptions, and questions for a lender or small business adviser. Use ranges until evidence improves them.

Output: a first cost and funding question list.
WEEK 4

Choose the next step

Review what the research changed. Pick one route to investigate further, one person to talk with, and a weekly planning habit.

Output: a 30 day follow on plan.
03 / Ownership path

Separate the dream from the next decision

A B&B and a large hotel involve different capital, operations, and teams. This path keeps the first decision small enough to investigate.

  1. Explore the concept. Choose a possible scale and guest experience to research.
  2. Compare routes. Existing business, property conversion, or experience in operations first.
  3. Check the constraints. Research location specific lodging rules, property costs, financing, and practical operating skills.
  4. Gather evidence. Talk to owners, operators, advisers, and potential guests; compare what you learn against your assumptions.
  5. Decide the next commitment. Make any purchase or business formation decisions only after the facts are clearer.

Questions to answer before choosing

ExperienceWhat hospitality work or training would help you learn the daily business?
MoneyWhat size of project could you realistically investigate, and which funding questions need an expert answer?
PlaceWhich region and guest type should you research first?
PeopleWho could explain ownership, operations, and financing from lived experience?
04 / Practice before commitment

Run a small hospitality exercise

Pick one exercise this week. The goal is to learn about the work and the numbers, then update the plan.

Example: Imagine a four room B&B on a busy weekend. A guest checks in late, one room needs attention, and breakfast starts at 8 a.m. Write who handles each job, how long it takes, and what support would be needed.

Three possible first experiments

  1. Interview an owner or manager about their first year.
  2. Draft a sample guest journey from booking to checkout.
  3. Make a paper budget with rough ranges and five questions to verify.

Choose one. Keep observations separate from guesses.
