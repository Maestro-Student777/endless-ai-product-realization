THE CORRECTION

Two connected ideas, with separate books

Iris’s hotel

Rooms, meals, guest service, retreats and private hospitality experiences. Hotel financing and operating results stand on their own.

The Forest Commons

Public-benefit trails, stewardship, access agreements, sponsored events, training and forest work. Each public or sponsored project has its own agreement, budget and obligations.

A municipal or DNR program would support an eligible public project and applicant. It should not be counted as money for the private hotel. A city, county, tribe, nonprofit, trail group or agency may become a real partner only after it agrees to the role.

01 · PLACE

Four places inside one forest

Guest woodland

Quiet walks, art, stargazing, river days and guided experiences connected to the hotel.

Community trail

A defined route that could host city-sponsored days, conservation learning, or a formal access partnership.

Working forest

Habitat care, timber management and restoration planned with a forester and separated from guest activity.

Forest Employees yard

Tools, safety meetings, process instruction and practical training for the people who care for the place.

Event commons

Small outdoor classes, maker days, sponsor-supported equipment demonstrations and community celebrations.

Protected quiet

Areas intentionally left alone for water, wildlife, recovery and the long-term health of the forest.

03 · PEOPLE

A community of Forest Employees

These are job pathways to design and test, not promised positions. One person may learn several roles as the project grows.

Trail steward

Walk the route, document hazards, clear approved debris, update signs and record completion.

Habitat crew

Follow a forester or conservation plan, identify invasive species, protect water and keep a field log.

Guest guide

Welcome people, explain boundaries, lead a screen-light activity and close the loop safely.

River and access crew

Check conditions, equipment and access points; work only within training, licensing and partner rules.

Event maker

Set up a small experience, work with sponsors, capture consented media and restore the site afterward.

Process apprentice

Learn one repeatable task, demonstrate it, collect proof and teach the next person with supervision.

04 · TEACHING

Teach it like a practical dad would

The feeling is warm, direct and useful: one real task, shown clearly, with the mistake people usually make. A short vertical video can carry the lesson, but the instruction should also work as a printable card. This is a style cue, not a TikTok affiliation.

  1. Show the finished result. “Here is what good looks like.”
  2. Name the task and why it matters.
  3. Show three actions. Keep each one visible and concrete.
  4. Call out the safety check and the common mistake.
  5. Show the proof. Photo, measurement, checklist or supervisor sign-off.
  6. Invite the learner to try it and record what happened.
05 · REAL PROGRAMS

Examples of how public access can work

Minnesota examples are useful models until Iris chooses a location. The Federal Recreational Trail Program requires a government sponsor and can support eligible trail development, restoration and education. The Walk-In Access program is a voluntary private-land program for defined public recreational access; its rules, seasons and eligible land uses are specific. The Outdoor Recreation Grant Program is for local government applicants and carries public-recreation obligations.

Those examples show several structures: a government-sponsored trail, a limited access agreement, or a public park project. None automatically turns private land into a state park, and none should be promised before the land, applicant, public use, restrictions and current application window are verified.
